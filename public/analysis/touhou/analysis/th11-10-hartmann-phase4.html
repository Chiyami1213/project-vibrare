Verdict

Theme 1 Head · mm.1–2 Usable with Care

2 bars of 7/8, E minor, quarter = 158
  • RRange E♭4–D5, a seventh, inside the octave Marpurg asks for (Mann p. 163). Starts on ⑤ on the downbeat. S
  • REnds on B♭ (♯④, as A♯) on the last eighth of bar 2, leaning into ⑤. Marpurg wants the theme to end on a strong beat (p. 164); here the arrival on B is the downbeat where the answer enters, so the countersubject takes it. I
  • RAnswer: tonal at the start only. The subject falls ⑤–③–① (B–G–E), and Marpurg answers a theme on ⑤ on ① (p. 167): E for F♯. The rest stays real, since the theme does not leave E minor (p. 166). I
  • GEvery bar is broken chords (E minor, then E♭ major over the bass E♭). Marpurg keeps arpeggios out of keyboard fugue subjects (p. 162), so this is an instrumental subject, best fast and in three voices. I
  • GStretto only late: octave below or unison after 12 eighths. I
  • GA new countersubject inverts at the octave against both answers. I

Theme 2 · mm.17–21 Not a Subject

4/4, no fixed key
  • REach bar is two broken chords in bare fifths and octaves (E–B–E, F–B–F). Marpurg rules out arpeggio themes (p. 162). S
  • GNo key: melody and bass climb by half steps (E–F, F♯–G, G♯–A), so there is no tonic and dominant for an answer to exchange (pp. 164–166). I
  • GIt suits an episode: a chromatic sequence that can lead from one key to another. I

Theme 3 Head · mm.51–52 Good Subject

1¼ bars of 4/4, A minor, quarter = 158
  • RRange A4–A5, the octave (Mann p. 163). Starts on ① on the downbeat and ends on ③ on the next downbeat, with no cadence (pp. 163–164). S
  • RAnswer: tonal. The skip ①–⑤ (A down to E) is answered ⑤–① (E down to A), where a real answer has B (p. 167). I
  • GShort: five beats. Marpurg: a theme is long enough “whenever it expresses a clear thought,” and short themes can return more often (p. 162). I
  • GClose stretto: an octave lower after one beat, with the leader’s last note changed. I
  • GThe arrangement’s bass F–G–A is a harmonic bass, not a countersubject; a new countersubject passes both ways. I

Theme 1 as a Subject mm.1–2

Notes
How it is built. The subject (mm.1–2) is answered a fourth lower, in B minor, from bar 3. The new countersubject takes the downbeat where the subject would arrive on B, and its first note follows the answer: G over the tonal answer’s E, D over the real answer’s F♯. B itself would make a fifth or fourth with either first note, which fails when the parts swap (Fux, Mann p. 108). The countersubject moves in quarters where the answer has broken chords, and its B♭ in bar 4 agrees with the answer’s chromatic chord. “Subject Below” puts the subject an octave and the countersubject two octaves lower: both layouts pass. I
Notes
Only a late stretto. The second voice enters an octave lower on the sixth eighth of bar 2, so the statements overlap for five eighths. Every closer entry clashes with the E♭ chord in bar 2 (a major seventh or tritone on the beat; see the table). After its subject the leader continues freely (A–G–B–G–B♭–G). I
Notes
A ground, not a countersubject. E–F–E–E♭ (①–♭②–①–♯⑦) fits the theme as written, with only a direct fifth at the start of bar 2. Its fifths (bar 1, eighth 1; bar 2, eighths 1, 4 and 7) become fourths when the parts swap (red circles), so it cannot change places with the subject. It suits a prelude on a ground bass. I

Theme 3 as a Subject mm.51–52

Notes
How it is built. The subject (m.51 and the downbeat of m.52) is answered an octave and a fourth lower, in E minor, on the next downbeat; its last note is shortened to an eighth so the countersubject can move on. Against the tonal answer’s A the countersubject has C (a third); against the real answer’s B it has D. “Subject Below” puts the answer an octave higher and the subject and countersubject an octave lower. Both layouts pass. I
Notes
A close stretto. The second voice enters an octave lower on beat 2. The statements overlap for four beats without a fault; only the leader’s last note must change (E for C), or it makes octaves with the follower’s B–C. The table lists other clean entries, such as the octave above after two beats. I
Notes
A harmonic bass. F–G–A (♭⑥–♭⑦–①, VI–VII–i) is ZUN’s progression, not a counterpoint: against the theme alone it makes a major seventh (E over F, beat 2½) and a fourth on beat 4 (C over G). In the arrangement the other parts fill these into chords. I
