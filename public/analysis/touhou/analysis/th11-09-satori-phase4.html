Verdict

Theme 1 Head · mm.1–2 Good Subject

1¾ bars, B minor, quarter = 152
  • RRange B4–G5, a minor sixth, inside the octave Marpurg asks for. Starts on ① on the downbeat and ends on ⑤ on beat 3, a strong beat, with no perfect cadence (Mann pp. 163–164). S
  • RAnswer: tonal. The subject skips ⑤–① (F♯ down to B) in its first bar, and Marpurg answers that skip with ①–⑤ (Mann p. 167). The answer has B where a real answer has C♯. I
  • GThe tail is two broken chords (B minor, then G major). Marpurg keeps arpeggios out of keyboard and vocal fugue subjects (p. 162), so this is a lively, instrumental subject, best fast and in three voices. I
  • GStretto works only at the octave or unison after 3 beats. I
  • GA new countersubject inverts at the octave against the subject and both answers. I

Theme 2 Head · mm.26–29 Good Subject

3½ bars, B minor, quarter = 152
  • RRange B4–F♯5, a fifth. Starts on ⑤ and ends on ① with the sigh ③–♭②–① on beat 3. The ending is a melodic close but not a perfect cadence in the arrangement (the bass has F♯), which Marpurg allows (Mann p. 163). S
  • RAnswer: tonal. F♯–B–F♯ (⑤–①–⑤) is answered B–F♯–B (Mann p. 167). The chromatic E♯ and C♮ become B♯ and G♮ (p. 170). I
  • GLong notes and a slow harmonic rhythm leave room for an active countersubject. Marpurg warns that plain long notes in a slow tempo become “unbearable” (p. 161), so keep the tempo moving. I
  • GStretto works only late: an octave lower after 9 beats, with the follower’s first note halved (allowed for an answer, p. 165). I
  • GThe arrangement’s bass is not a countersubject, but a new one keeps its falling G♯–G–F♯. I

Other Material Episodes

  • GTheme 1 and Theme 2 do not combine. No distance or octave gives two clean parts, so a double fugue on both themes as written is out. I
  • GTheme 2, phrase b (mm.30–33), climbs in a sequence (1 + 1 + 2 bars) and ends on the leading tone over V. That suits an episode better than a subject. I
  • GTheme 1, phrase 1 has a second candidate in its descent E–D–C♯ (motif c, m.3), which could be imitated in episodes. I

Theme 1 as a Subject mm.1–2

Notes
How it is built. The subject (mm.1–2 of the arrangement) is followed by the answer a fifth higher, against a new countersubject. “Subject Below” swaps the parts: the countersubject is now under the answer, which tests double counterpoint at the octave. Both answers and both layouts pass the check: no parallel fifths or octaves, no unprepared dissonance, and no fifths between the parts, since a fifth turns into a fourth when the parts swap (Fux, Mann p. 108). The countersubject keeps to quarters and eighths where the answer has 16ths, and starts with a 7–6 tie over the bar line (E over F♯ resolving to D), which becomes 2–3 when the parts swap. I
Notes
The only close stretto. The second voice enters an octave lower on beat 4 of bar 1. The subjects overlap for 4½ beats with no fault. The leader’s last note is free (F♯ shortened, then E–D–E–D–C♯–B), and the follower ends with A♯–B. Apart from the same entry at the unison, every other distance and interval breaks a rule before the leader finishes; see the table below. I

Theme 2 as a Subject mm.26–29

Notes
How it is built. The subject (mm.26–29) is answered a fifth higher in F♯ minor. The chromatic notes come across as B♯ and G♮, as Marpurg prescribes for chromatic themes (Mann p. 170). The new countersubject moves in quarters against the long notes and falls through the arrangement’s G♯–G–F♯ on the strong beats of bars 2–3 (D♯–D–C♯ against the answer). With the tonal answer, the countersubject’s first bar must change (G♯–A–G♯ instead of A–F♯–E♯), because the adjusted B would clash with it; with the real answer it fits unchanged. Both layouts pass the check. I
Notes
A late stretto. The second voice enters an octave lower on beat 2 of bar 3, with its first note shortened to a quarter. The two statements overlap for 7 beats without a fault; the leader ends on C♯ instead of B so that it agrees with the E♯ below it. Closer entries all hit a dissonance on a beat (usually E against F♯ at bar 3). I
Notes
Why the arrangement’s bass is not a countersubject. B–G♯–G–F♯ is a harmonic bass: as two-part counterpoint it makes a tritone (C♯ over G, bar 3) and a fourth (B over F♯, bar 4), and its fifths in bars 1 and 3 become fourths when the parts swap (red circles). In four-part harmony those spots are normal chords (iiø⁴₃ and i⁴₃ in the Phase 2 reading), so the line still works as a ground for a prelude. The new countersubject passes both ways. I
