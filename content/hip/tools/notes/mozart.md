# L. Mozart, Treatise on the Fundamental Principles of Violin Playing (1756; trans. Knocker) — CROSS-REFERENCE ONLY
## Ch. IV Order of up and down strokes (pp. ~72–95)
- Rule 1: first note of each bar (if no rest) with a DOWN stroke, even if two down strokes follow; third crotchet in common time also down; exceptions only in quickest tempo. → strong beats = down-bow (weight); analogue: flute tonguing "ti" on strong notes / keyboard weight on strong beats.
- After crotchet rest → up stroke; last note of a bar and the upbeat → UP stroke (anacrusis = up/lighter).
- Dotted pairs within one stroke: bow lifted at the dot, short note deferred "till the last moment" (late short note).
- Four notes with 2nd & 4th dotted: slurred in pairs; dotted note accented and sustained smoothly.
- One long + two quick → two quick slurred in one stroke.
## Ch. V Tone production (pp. 96–102)
- Every tone, even the strongest, begins & ends with a small softness, otherwise noise. Divisions of the bow: (1) soft–loud–soft (messa di voce) for long notes in Adagio — like a singer sustaining a long note without breath; (2) loud→soft for short sustained notes in quick tempo; (3) soft→loud; (4) several swells in one stroke; also perfectly even tone in a slow stroke.
- Even tone quality in forte and piano; piano ≠ whispering; avoid open strings in solo; play on one string for consistent colour.
- "Singing is at all times the aim of every instrumentalist." Fit many notes in one stroke; "A singer who during every short phrase stopped, took a breath, and specially stressed first this note, then that note, would unfailingly move everyone to laughter"; break only where expression or the divisions of the phrase (incisiones, distinctiones — rhetoric) demand. → the bowing ↔ breathing analogy the owner wants.
## Ch. XII Reading music correctly and good execution (pp. ~215–228)
- "Everything depends on good execution"; bad players embellish foolishly, ignore affect; adagio betrays them. Reading good masters rightly (observe all marks, throw oneself into the affect, apply ties, slides, accents, forte/piano tastefully) is more artistic than playing difficult solos. Good orchestral (ensemble) player worth more than mere soloist: learn to accompany before playing solo.
- Before playing, study the piece: character, tempo, kind of movement, difficult places; find and render the composer's affect; be moved yourself.
- Observe written piano/forte exactly; also change p/f of one's own accord ("Light and Shade"); never play "like a hurdy-gurdy" in one tone.
- Notes raised by ♯/♮ played rather more strongly, then diminish; sudden flattening also forte. Minims among short notes accented then relaxed (fp); accent without lifting the bow, tone dies away.
- Strong beats = Nota buona (first of bar; half-bar; etc.) get chief stress unless marked otherwise; "good notes" at smaller levels get small accents with moderation.
- Slurred pairs: first accented, louder and a little longer; second slurred on quietly and somewhat late. Longer slurs: first accented and sustained, rest diminishing without accent. Where nothing is marked, the player must apply slurring/detaching tastefully.
- Syncopated accent on last beat slurred to the next downbeat: no new stress (play as one minim); in lively pieces accent on the highest note.
- Notes tied across the barline: no accent at the barline; quiet sustain. Three degrees of accent: strong (fp tutti), moderate (important notes), barely audible (others). Accompanying a soloist: forte in moderation; merry passages light, short, lifted strokes; slow sad pieces long strokes simply and tenderly; accompaniment crotchets in 2/4 & 3/4 almost as quavers (not sleepy) but crotchet heard more than quaver.
- Accompanist keeps strict tempo under a true virtuoso's tempo rubato (anticipating/postponing notes) — matches CPE's rubato (one hand against the bar, the other strict).
- Chords played smartly together; short notes after a dot or short rest LATE and rapid; upbeat groups slurred into the next beat; ensemble players watch each other and the leader.
## Ch. IX Appoggiaturas (cross-ref)
- Appoggiaturas demanded "by Nature herself" to bind notes, make melody song-like (even peasants). RULE WITHOUT EXCEPTION: appoggiatura never separated from its main note, taken in the same stroke (= slurred; flute: one breath/tongue stroke; keyboard: legato into the resolution). Belongs to the FOLLOWING note.
- Long descending appoggiatura before crotchet/quaver/semiquaver = half the value (agrees with CPE/Quantz); before dotted notes = value of the note (the dotted value); before a minim in some metres = three quarters of the minim; sustain through rests/pauses if needed. Don't add another appoggiatura to written-out ones (exaggerated).
## Ch. X Trill (cross-ref)
- Trill = alternation of a note with its upper second (whole or half step per key — attend to modulation); avoid trills on augmented seconds (use another ornament). May begin at once from the upper note, or be prepared by a longer descending appoggiatura, or by ascending ribattuta (end of cadenzas, free time); end with turn (Nachschlag) most naturally. Short trills: quick appoggiatura + turn. Four speeds: slow (sad, slow pieces), medium (lively but moderate), rapid, accelerating (cadenzas).
