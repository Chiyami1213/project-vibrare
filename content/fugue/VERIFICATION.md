# Verification: Fugue pack

## Theory tab (Mann, *The Study of Fugue*)
- **Source.** The PDF has no text layer. All 179 page images were OCR'd (Tesseract) and read in full: Part One chapters I–V, Fux, Marpurg, Albrechtsberger (introduction, chorale fugue, canon), and Martini (introduction and the seven models). The notes on the classic texts and the musical examples were skimmed.
- **Wording.** Content is paraphrased. Quotations are short and marked.
- **Rules vs guidelines.** "Rules" are what a teacher states as binding: Fux's plan and his double-counterpoint prohibitions, Marpurg's definitions and answer rules, Albrechtsberger's canon requirements. "Guidelines" are advice, stated exceptions, and Mann's commentary.
- **Page numbers.** These refer to the Dover 1987 edition and were derived from the scans (two book pages per image). They are accurate to about ±1 page; check any you cite.
- **Tools.**
  - The answer generator was tested on its presets. The D-mode subject gets a real answer in the mode. The C-major subject that rises to the dominant gets a tail mutation, with only the last note answered at the 4th. The D-minor head D–A is answered A–D.
  - The answer generator applies Marpurg's rules mechanically. Marpurg himself lists exceptions (⑤ answered by ②, Phrygian endings, chromatic themes), so treat its output as a first suggestion.
  - The stretto finder and the invertible-counterpoint calculator count only two-voice intervals. The guide says so on the page.
- **Examples.** The two notated examples are models written for this guide and labelled as such. No example of Mann's is reproduced.

## Analysis tab
- **Roberts (2004).** Used in full for the method, the statistics, the critiques, and the cases (BWV 847, 578, 533, 545, 547). Roberts does not state a rhetorical/literal verdict for BWV 545; the data says "not stated".
- **Badura-Skoda (1993).** Only Ch. 9 (the structure as a whole) and Ch. 10 (WTC I D♯ minor fugue) are used, as you asked: only fugue-relevant content.
- **Bodky (1960).** Only the parts that bear on reading fugue subjects are used: articulation slurs, affect and key, and symbolism. Key symbolism is presented with Bodky's own caution.
- **Gedalge.** Only the one-page "Plan for an Academic Fugue" table you supplied.
- **C. P. E. Bach, *Essay*.** The OCR is 86% complete; the rest is still running for the HIP pack. The *Essay* contains no fugue analysis, so only three things are used:
  - the organ-point chapter: pedal points near the end of fugues, with stretto over the dominant pedal;
  - the accompaniment rules for a fugue subject in the bass, and octave doubling for entries;
  - Mitchell's introduction on the Bach circle's Rameau-style analyses of WTC fugues (Kirnberger).
