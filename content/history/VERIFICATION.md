# Verification: History pack

## Source and method
- The source is Christensen (ed.), *The Cambridge History of Western Music Theory* (CUP 2002).
- Every chapter was read at survey level: section headings, the topic sentence of each paragraph, and the full first and last paragraphs. That is about 63,000 of 340,000 words. All articles are therefore marked `status: "survey"`.
- Names, dates and treatise years were checked against the book's text by search. Corrections made in that check:
  - Westphal: the 19th-century revival of Greek metrics, not an application to Bach.
  - Réti spelled with the accent.
  - Mersmann's work title removed.
  - Nothing after 2002 is attributed to the book.

## What remains here
- **Narrative.** Summary and key points for all 32 articles.
- **Indexes.** 125 people, 132 works and 119 terms. Each term shows where it is taught.
- **Quiz.** 45 history questions; 21 technical ones moved with their technique.
- **Rules.** Only two articles keep rules: rhythmic notation (Franco, Ars nova), which is notation history, and twelve-tone theory, which no other tab covers.
- **Organum article.** Its key points were rewritten as history, because the rules moved to the Counterpoint tab.

## Choices made by me (the pack author)
- **Eras and threads.** The structure of eras and threads is mine; the book is organized by tradition, not by date.
- **Article spans.** Article spans are approximate, so that the articles can be placed on the era map.
- **Twelve-tone matrix.** The twelve-tone matrix stays here until a 20th-century techniques tab exists.

## Open questions
1. **Repertoire.** Should composers and works of music (not just treatises) come from a second source, such as a music-history survey? The `people`, `works` and `events` model is ready for them.
2. **Eras.** Do you want the era boundaries changed? For example, the Renaissance is set at 1430–1600 and the Classical era at 1750–1820.
