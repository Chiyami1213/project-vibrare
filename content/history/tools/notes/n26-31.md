## Ch26 Schenker (Drabkin, 812–846)
- Schenker (1868–1935): Neue musikalische Theorien und Phantasien: Harmonielehre (1906), Kontrapunkt I (1910), II (1922), Der freie Satz (1935); Der Tonwille (1921–24), Das Meisterwerk in der Musik (1925–30), Fünf Urlinie-Tafeln (1932); editions (Beethoven sonatas), Erläuterungsausgaben; performance. Key concept: hierarchy. Four concepts: Stufe (scale step: harmonic entity governing a span; Bach "Buß und Reu" analysis in Harmonielehre), Auskomponierung (composing-out), Urlinie/Ursatz (fundamental line descending from 3, 5 or 8 to 1 over Bassbrechung I–V–I; "chord of nature"), levels (background Hintergrund, middleground Mittelgrund, foreground Vordergrund); transformations: Zug (linear progression), Ausfaltung, Übergreifen, Unterbrechung (interruption 3–2 || 3–2–1), mixture, Koppelung, reaching-over, neighbour note, concealed repetition (verborgene Wiederholung). Fundamental structure arrhythmic. Organicism; genius; German chauvinism; American reception (Jonas, Salzer Structural Hearing 1952, Oster, Forte; Rothstein "Americanization").
## Ch27 Music and rhetoric (McCreless, 847–879)
- Rhetoric: Corax & Tisias Syracuse c. 485 BCE; Aristotle; Rhetorica ad Herennium (c. 85 BCE); Cicero; Quintilian Institutio oratoria (92–94 CE). Five canons: inventio, dispositio, elocutio, memoria, pronuntiatio/actio; figures. Musica poetica: Listenius Musica (1537) (musica theorica, practica, poetica — opus perfectum et absolutum); Dressler (1563); Burmeister Hypomnematum (1599), Musica autoschediastike (1601), Musica poetica (1606): figures (harmonic: noema, anaploce, fuga realis, hypallage; melodic: palilogia, climax; etc.), analysis of Lasso "In me transierunt" by rhetorical parts (exordium, body, ending). Nucius (1613), Thuringus (1624), Bernhard (Tractatus c. 1660: stylus gravis/luxurians; figures as licensed dissonance: passus duriusculus, saltus duriusculus, superjectio, anticipatio…), Kircher Musurgia (1650), Walther (1708), Mattheson Der vollkommene Capellmeister (1739): dispositio parts exordium, narratio, propositio, confirmatio, confutatio, peroratio; Affektenlehre; Forkel; Scheibe vs Birnbaum on Bach. Koch's punctuation. Decline c. 1800; revival: Schering, Unger (1941), "Figurenlehre" historiography and critiques (Vickers; Bartel Musica poetica 1997). Rhetoric & modern analysis (topics: Ratner Classic Music 1980; Agawu; Hatten).
## Ch28 Form (Burnham, 880–906)
- Formenlehre tradition, sonata form central. Koch Versuch vol. 3 (1793): first-movement as expanded binary (Anlage, Ausführung), punctuation; Galeazzi Elementi (1796) sonata parts (motivo, passo caratteristico…). Reicha Traité de haute composition (1824–26): grande coupe binaire, analysis of Figaro overture; "développement". Marx Die Lehre von der musikalischen Komposition (1837–47): Satz vs Gang; Liedform, rondo forms 1–5, sonata form (Hauptsatz, Seitensatz, Schlusssatz; first part, second part (Durchführung), third part); "Sonatenform" coined; Czerny School of Practical Composition (c. 1848). Riemann; Lorenz Das Geheimnis der Form bei Richard Wagner (1924–33: Bar & arch forms). 20th c.: Tovey, Schoenberg (Fundamentals of Musical Composition 1967: sentence, period), Ratz (1951), Rosen Sonata Forms (1980/88), Caplin Classical Form (1998: formal functions), Hepokoski & Darcy sonata theory (deformation; 1997 article; Elements 2006). Schenker's view of form from Ursatz (Der freie Satz).
## Ch29 Thematic and motivic analysis (Dunsby, 907–926)
- Theme & motive vocabulary since 17th c. Schoenberg: Grundgestalt, developing variation (Brahms the Progressive 1947); Reti The Thematic Process in Music (1951; Beethoven Op. 135 analysis); Keller functional analysis (wordless; Mozart chamber music); Schenker's motivic parallelisms; set-theory motivic analysis; semiotics: Ruwet paradigmatic analysis (Langage, musique, poésie 1972; medieval flagellant song), Nattiez (tripartition: poietic/neutral/esthesic), paradigm vs syntagm; Lidov; Epstein Beyond Orpheus (1979).
## Ch30 Energetics (Rothfarb, 927–955)
- Motion as quintessential attribute of music (ancient: Aristoxenus dynameis; medieval appetitus of intervals; oration metaphor in Baroque). 19th c.: Marx (Hauptsatz energy), Fétis & Vivier (tendency tones; attraction), Lipps (psychology of rhythm/tendency), Riemann (dynamics/agogics). Core: August Halm (Von zwei Kulturen der Musik 1913: fugue vs sonata), Ernst Kurth (Grundlagen des linearen Kontrapunkts 1917 — Bach's melody as kinetic energy; Romantische Harmonik und ihre Krise in Wagners Tristan 1920; Bruckner 1925; Musikpsychologie 1931: Energie, Spannung, Bewegung), Mersmann (Angewandte Musikästhetik 1926; graph of Haydn sonata), Schenker partial. Legacy: Zuckerkandl Sound and Symbol (1956), Larson musical forces.
## Ch31 Psychology of music (Gjerdingen, 956–981)
- Mind responding to/imagining/performing/evaluating music. Basevi Studi sul armonia (1862) psychological roots. Locke, Hume, associationism; Herbart (tone relations), Comte positivism. German university: Wundt (Leipzig lab 1879; structuralism; Lorenz interval experiments vs Stumpf controversy (1890–91), Stumpf Tonpsychologie, Berlin Phonogrammarchiv). Gestalt (Wertheimer, Köhler, Koffka: grouping laws; Meyer). American functionalism: Seashore (b. Sweden; Psychology of Musical Talent 1919; Seashore tests; Psychology of Music 1938). Lipps–Meyer law (interval ending on power of 2). Meyer Emotion and Meaning in Music (1956: expectation, Gestalt, good continuation); Narmour implication–realization (1990, 1992); cognitive revolution: Deutsch, Krumhansl (tonal hierarchies 1990), Bregman auditory scene analysis (1990), Lerdahl & Jackendoff; Huron; schema theory (Gjerdingen).
