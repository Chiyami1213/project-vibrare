## Ch13 Tonal organization 17th c (Barnett, 407–455)
- Inherited gamut: litterae (letters) + voces (hexachord syllables); hexachords naturale (C), durum (G), molle (F); mutation; cantus durus (no flat) vs cantus mollis (one flat). Attempts to improve solmization: Banchieri (added syllable), bocedization (Calvisius, bo ce di ga lo ma ni), si. Penna Li primi albori (1672): imagine a signature-less "key" for transposed pieces. Modes in polyphony: authentic/plagal ambitus distributed across voices; fugue subjects & answers fit fifth/fourth species. Church keys/tones (tuoni ecclesiastici) for organists (Banchieri; 8 psalm-tone keys) vs 12 modes; cadence types (cadenza regolare, irregolare, peregrina; Bononcini Musico prattico 1673). Transposition & key signatures; English theory (Campion 1618 "air", Simpson, Playford; key by bass note and third). Toward major/minor: Masson Nouveau traité (1697) two modes; Charpentier key characters; Mattheson Neu-eröffnete Orchestre (1713) 24 keys & key characteristics; Heinichen musical circle (1711/1728).
## Ch14 Dualist tonal space (Klumpenhouwer, 456–476)
- 19th-c. German: thorough-bass tradition vs harmonic dualism. Hauptmann Die Natur der Harmonik und der Metrik (1853): Hegelian dialectic; octave (unity), fifth (duality), third (union); minor triad as inverse of major. Helmholtz undertones critique. Oettingen Harmoniesystem in dualer Entwickelung (1866): tonicity/phonicity; tables of fifths and thirds (Tonnetz-like). Riemann: Klang; major = Oberklang, minor = Unterklang (root = top note in minor); Schritte (steps) & Wechsel (changes): Quintschritt, Terzwechsel, Seitenwechsel, Leittonwechsel, etc.; Skizze (1880), Handbuch der Harmonielehre, Vereinfachte Harmonielehre (1893) function theory T S D; dissonant klangs (Scheinkonsonanzen, e.g., the 6/4 and added-sixth). Neo-Riemannian transformation (P, L, R) as modern legacy (Lewin, Hyer, Cohn).
## Ch15 Organum–discantus–contrapunctus (Fuller, 477–502)
- Organum c. 850–1030: Musica & Scolica enchiriadis: parallel organum at 5th/8ve, organum at the fourth with tritone avoidance (boundary tone, occursus convergence). Guido Micrologus (c. 1026) diaphony: oblique, occursus at phrase end; Ad organum faciendum (c. 1100), Montpellier treatise: contrary motion, new note-against-note practice. Discantus 13th c. (musica mensurabilis, Paris; Notre Dame codices W1, F, W2): Johannes de Garlandia De mensurabili musica (c. 1250) concords: perfect (unison, octave), medial (5th, 4th), imperfect (M3, m3); discords imperfect (M6, m7), medial (whole tone, m6), perfect (semitone, tritone, M7). Franco of Cologne Ars cantus mensurabilis (c. 1280). Contrapunctus 14th c.: punctus contra punctum; "Quilibet affectans" manual; rules: begin/end on perfect; no parallel perfects; contrary motion; imperfect → nearest perfect (directed progressions: M3→5, M6→8, m3→unison) with sharps (musica ficta "causa pulchritudinis"); Johannes de Muris attributed Ars contrapuncti; Prosdocimo Contrapunctus (1412). Mixed consonance types; 3rds/6ths as imperfect consonances.
## Ch16 Counterpoint pedagogy Renaissance (Schubert, 503–533)
- Four groups of authors: 15th-c. (Ugolino, Tinctoris Liber de arte contrapuncti 1477, Ramis, Gaffurio Practica 1496); 16th-c. Italian (Aron, Lusitano 1553, Vicentino 1555, Zarlino 1558, Artusi, Zacconi 1592/1622, Cerone 1613, Diruta), Spanish (Sancta Maria 1565, Montaños, Bermudo), German/English (Burmeister, Morley Plaine and Easie 1597, Campion). Contrapunctus simplex (note-against-note, consonances; formula lists to memorize for improvisation; movements from a sixth to octave etc.) vs diminutus/florid (Tinctoris's 8 rules: begin & end perfect, no parallel perfects, …, varietas). Modal considerations: Aron vs Bb in the bass; cadences per mode. Florid: dissonance passing on weak parts, syncopation (suspension). Motives (inventioni, passaggi); contrapunto fugato / imitation (Montaños "ymitacion general"); improvised canon (Lusitano). Double counterpoint at 8ve, 10th, 12th (Zarlino, Chiodino Arte pratica 1610). Four-part sonority: Sancta Maria's chord cycles; consonant 5/3 & 6/3. Missing link from first species to free imitation.
## Ch17 Performance theory (A. Cohen, 534–553)
- Score as object; practice vs theory since Greeks. Improvised counterpoint (Lusitano 1553, Vicentino 1555, Zarlino 1558; cantare super librum). Mersenne Harmonie universelle (1636): reports from performers (lute, viol, voice: ornaments, Bacilly). Thoroughbass: Viadana (1602) Cento concerti; Agazzari Del sonare sopra'l basso (1607): instruments as foundation vs ornament. Division manuals: Ortiz Trattado (1553), Simpson Division-Violist (1659). 18th c. treatises: Couperin L'art (1716), Quantz (1752), CPE Bach (1753/62), L. Mozart (1756): performance as theory. Partimento: Neapolitan practice of bass as basis of improvisation/composition; preluding. Recordings & electronic media; performance studies.
## Ch18 Steps to Parnassus (Bent, 554–602)
- Fux Gradus ad Parnassum (1725; Fux 1660–1741): dialogue Aloysius (Palestrina) – Josephus; Latin; frontispiece; Italian trans. Salita al Parnasso (1761); German Mizler 1742; manuductio. Three motions (parallel, contrary, oblique) + four rules of progression (perfect→perfect: contrary/oblique; perfect→imperfect: any; imperfect→perfect: contrary/oblique; imperfect→imperfect: any). Precursors: Zarlino 1558 rules (no two perfect consonances in succession…; close approach to perfect), Banchieri Cartella (1614) paradigmata, Bononcini Musico prattico (1673), Berardi; species already in Diruta (1609), Banchieri, Zacconi, Bononcini; Fux's five species: 1 note against note, 2 two against one, 3 four against one, 4 ligature/syncopation, 5 florid. Successors: Martini Esemplare (1774–75), Albrechtsberger Gründliche Anweisung (1790), Cherubini (1835), Bellermann (1862), Jeppesen (1930/31), Schenker Kontrapunkt (1910/1922), Haydn/Beethoven study. Fugue & invertible counterpoint, modes in Gradus.
## Ch19 Twelve-tone theory (Covach, 603–627)
- Hauer (gnostic; 44 tropes discovered 1921; Vom Wesen des Musikalischen 1920; Zwölftontechnik 1926), Eimert Atonale Musiklehre (1924), Erwin Stein "Neue Formprinzipien" (1924) introduced Schoenberg's method; Grundgestalt; Schoenberg "Composition with Twelve Tones" (1941/1950). Twelve-tone notation proposals. 1930–45: Hill "Schoenberg's Tone-Rows and the Tonal System of the Future" (1936), Krenek Studies in Counterpoint (1940), Perle "Evolution of the Tone Row" (1941), twelve-tone modes; Leibowitz Schoenberg et son école (1947); Rufer (1952). Post-1945 Babbitt (combinatoriality, aggregate, invariance, time-point), Forte, Perle Twelve-Tone Tonality (1977); Mead; serial matrix P/I/R/RI. "Decline of twelve-tone theory?"
