## Ch5 Transmission (Bower, 136–167)
- Latin sources: Varro (liberal arts), Vitruvius, Quintilian, Censorinus De die natali (238), Calcidius (Timaeus commentary, 4th c.), Macrobius (Commentary on Dream of Scipio), Martianus Capella De nuptiis (before 439; fixed the seven liberal arts; Book IX Harmonia from Aristides), Augustine De musica (387–89; rhythm/metre & number), Boethius De institutione arithmetica & De institutione musica (c. 500; musica mundana/humana/instrumentalis; Pythagorean ratios; Greater Perfect System; monochord division; tones Nicomachus/Ptolemy), Cassiodorus Institutiones (monastery Vivarium), Isidore Etymologiae (sounds perish unless remembered "nisi enim ab homine memoria teneantur, pereunt").
- Quadrivium: arithmetic, music, geometry, astronomy (discrete vs continuous quantity; music = multitude related to another).
- Carolingian: Charlemagne (d. 814), Alcuin; glosses on Martianus & Boethius; Aurelian of Réôme Musica disciplina (mid-9th c., modes/octoechos), Regino of Prüm Epistola (c. 900), Musica & Scolica enchiriadis (late 9th c.; daseian notation; tetrachord T-S-T of four finals protus/deuterus/tritus/tetrardus; all tetrachords disjunct; 18 pitches; organum), Hucbald Musica (c. 900), Berno of Reichenau (after 1021), Hermannus Contractus (before 1054), Wilhelm of Hirsau.
- 10th–11th c. problem: fit chants into quantitative system; vitia (chromatic alterations); resolution in Dialogus de musica (formerly attr. Odo), Guido.
## Ch6 Medieval canonics (Herlinger, 168–192)
- Monochord (canon): systematic divisions of string lengths; 143 divisions catalogued by Meyer (Mensura monochordi), 13 chromatic/enharmonic.
- Magadis in utraque parte (by c. 1100): Greater Perfect System + synemmenon in Pythagorean tuning (tones 9:8, semitones 256:243, apotome 2187:2048). Dialogus de musica / Guido: simpler 9-part division, extension to c2.
- Pythagorean: pure 5ths & 8ves; thirds/sixths dissonant (81:64 ditone); archetypical medieval cadence (major 6th→8ve, 3rd→unison).
- Just tuning: Ramis de Pareia Musica practica (1482): just monochord, eight-syllable solmization (psal-li-tur per vo-ces is-tas); Burzio's attack (Musices opusculum 1487); Spataro's defence (1491). Earlier 15th-c. just/schismatic divisions (Divide primo: Gb…G fifths, pure triads via schisma).
- Chromatic/enharmonic monochords after Boethius 4.6; Marchetto of Padua Lucidarium (1317/18) dividing the whole tone into 5 dieses (chromatic semitone 4/5 of a tone) — possibly influenced. Prosdocimo de' Beldomandi Parvus tractatulus (1413) monochord with 17 notes.
## Ch7 Tuning and temperament (Rasch, 193–222)
- Methods: string lengths → root extraction (16th c.) → logarithms (17th c.) → cents (A. J. Ellis, 19th c.).
- Pythagorean (fifths 3:2, ditone 408 c; Pythagorean comma 23.46 c), just intonation (Ramis, Fogliano, Salinas De musica libri septem 1577; 5:4 third; syntonic comma 81:80, 21.5 c), meantone (fifths narrowed 1/4 syntonic comma: pure major thirds; Zarlino 2/7-comma 1558; Aron 1523 describes ¼-comma practice; Salinas 1/3 & 1/4), limitations of wolf fifth G#–Eb and 12-key keyboards (split keys), equal temperament (Stevin c. 1600 root extraction; Mersenne; Faulhaber logarithms; Sauveur 1697/1701 merides & logarithmic systems; 43-division). Well-tempered/irregular (Werckmeister 1691, Neidhardt, Kirnberger, Vallotti, Young). 19th-c. dominance of ET; 31-ET (Huygens, Fokker).
