## Ch20 Rhythmic notation (Busse Berger, 628–656)
- Chant not uniform but unmeasured notation. Modal notation (13th c., Notre Dame): Johannes de Garlandia De mensurabili musica (c. 1250): six rhythmic modes (1 LB, 2 BL, 3 LBB, 4 BBL, 5 LL, 6 BBB) — modes 1, 2, 6 "recti"; ligature patterns (3+2+2… for mode 1), contextual reading; with/without text. Anonymous IV. Franco of Cologne Ars cantus mensurabilis (c. 1280): note shapes with fixed values (duplex long, long, breve, semibreve), perfection = 3 tempora, imperfection and alteration rules; ligatures with propriety & perfection. Ars nova (early 14th c.): Philippe de Vitry (Ars nova treatise attr., c. 1320), Johannes de Muris Notitia artis musicae (1321): minim; modus, tempus, prolatio each perfect/imperfect; mensuration signs (circle/half-circle, dot); red notation. Italian trecento: Marchetto Pomerium (c. 1318–19): divisiones (quaternaria, senaria, octonaria, duodenaria); via naturae (semibreve positions). Ars subtilior; proportions (Tinctoris Proportionale 1473–74); Roman system of weights analogy; tactus (Gaffurio, Ramis): beat as measure of time.
## Ch21 Rhythm 18th–19th c. (Caplin, 657–694)
- From mensural system (tactus) to modern meter: bar lines, time signatures, accents. 17th c. gradual change (proportions → time signatures). Accent tied to meter: good/bad notes (quantitas intrinseca; nota buona/cattiva; Walther), struck vs passing. Mattheson Der vollkommene Capellmeister (1739): Rhythmik/Klangfüsse (poetic feet: spondee, iamb, dactyl…), Metrik. Kirnberger Die Kunst des reinen Satzes (1771–79) & Schulz: tempo giusto, meter types, compound meters, Takt as hearing units (Einschnitt, Abschnitt). Koch Versuch (1782–93): phrase (Einschnitt, Absatz, Periode), punctuation, extension and compression, rhythmic regularity (4-bar phrases), Taktteile (inner value). Momigny (cadence = upbeat→downbeat, iambic). 19th c.: Hauptmann (1853) meter dialectic (two/three, upbeat), Lussy Traité de l'expression musicale (1874; accents métriques/rythmiques/pathétiques), Westphal (Greek rhythmics applied to Bach), Riemann Musikalische Dynamik und Agogik (1884), System der musikalischen Rhythmik und Metrik (1903): upbeat-oriented motives (Auftaktigkeit), 8-bar schema (Achttaktigkeit), dynamics & agogics.
## Ch22 Rhythm 20th c. (London, 695–725)
- Motion & energy: Kurth, Neumann (Die Zeitgestalten 1959), Hasty Meter as Rhythm (1997: projection). Schenker: fundamental structure arrhythmic; rhythm arises in middleground; Schachter "Rhythm and Linear Analysis" (durational reduction 1976–87); Rothstein Phrase Rhythm in Tonal Music (1989); Yeston Stratification of Musical Rhythm (1976: levels, consonance/dissonance), Krebs metrical dissonance (grouping G and displacement D). Cooper & Meyer Rhythmic Structure of Music (1960: five poetic groups iamb, anapest, trochee, dactyl, amphibrach; architectonic levels). Lerdahl & Jackendoff GTTM (1983): grouping vs meter, well-formedness/preference rules, time-span & prolongational reductions. Kramer The Time of Music (1988: hypermeter, linear/nonlinear time). Serial/composers' rhythm: Messiaen Technique (1944: added values, non-retrogradable rhythms), Stockhausen "…how time passes…" (1957: formant spectrum), Babbitt time-point system (1962). Lewin rhythm transformations; cognitive (Povel/Essens; London Hearing in Time).
## Ch23 Tonality (Hyer, 726–752)
- Term first used by Choron (1810: arrangement of dominant & subdominant around the tonic; tonalité moderne vs ancient); popularized by Fétis (1830s–40s; Traité complet 1844): tonality = sum of necessary relations among scale notes; dominant seventh birth in Monteverdi "Stracciami pur il core" (1592); revelation in Bois de Boulogne 1831; types unitonique, transitonique, pluritonique, omnitonique; metaphysical attraction. Many senses: (1) pitch-centric organization generally, (8) arrangement around a referential tonic in European music c. 1680–1910. Tonic as presence/plenitude; grids/Tonnetz (Euler, Weber key table, Oettingen, Riemann); function theory; Schenker's tonal space (tonality = composing-out of the tonic triad; mixture; Stufen); Schoenberg Structural Functions of Harmony (monotonality, regions chart). Critiques; atonality ("atonal"). Neo-Riemannian.
## Ch24 Rameau & 18th-c. harmony (Lester, 753–777)
- Preconditions: 5/3 and 6/3 as building blocks (Zarlino, Lippius Synopsis musicae novae 1612: "trias harmonica"), thoroughbass manuals, rules of the octave (Campion 1618 progression rule; Delair 1690; Campion F. 1716 règle de l'octave), cadential directionality (mid-16th c. clausulae). Rameau (1683–1764) Traité de l'harmonie (1722): Book I chords from divisions of a string (fundamental, inversion — triads and sevenths as invertible chords), Book II fundamental bass (basse fondamentale) moving by 5ths and 3rds; dissonance demands resolution; cadences parfaite, irrégulière (IV–I), rompue/interrompue (evaded); chords by supposition (9th, 11th), double emploi (ii6/5 as IV add6 / ii7). Nouveau système (1726), Génération harmonique (1737: corps sonore, harmonic series), Démonstration (1750). Analysis of Lully's "Enfin il est en ma puissance" (Armide). Followers/critics: d'Alembert Élémens (1752), Marpurg (translation/critique), Sorge, Kirnberger (essential vs inessential dissonances; Die wahren Grundsätze 1773 with Schulz analyzing Bach), Vogler, Tartini (third sound), Rousseau.
## Ch25 19th-c. harmonic theory, Austro-German (Bernstein, 778–811)
- Rameau's legacy: scale-degree generation. Vogler (1749–1814): Roman numerals for degrees (1776/1802 Tonwissenschaft), Mehrdeutigkeit (multiple meaning), regions limited. Gottfried Weber (1779–1839) Versuch einer geordneten Theorie der Tonsetzkunst (1817–21): Roman numerals (upper/lowercase), key-relation table (Tonnetz of keys), multiple meaning; analysis of the "Dissonance" Quartet (Mozart K. 465) intro. Sechter (1788–1867) Die Grundsätze der musikalischen Komposition (1853–54): fundamental bass progressions by fifth, thirds; stepwise roots via hidden intermediate root (Zwitterakkord), diatonic-chromatic system; Bruckner's teacher. Mayrberger (1878) analysis of Tristan Prelude (Zwitterakkord). Hauptmann; Riemann function theory (T, S, D; Parallel, Leittonwechsel); Schenker Harmonielehre (1906); Schoenberg Harmonielehre (1911) & regions (Structural Functions 1954). Bruckner, Louis & Thuille. Tristan chord interpretations.
