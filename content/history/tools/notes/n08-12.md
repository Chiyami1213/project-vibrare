## Ch8 Harmonics in the scientific revolution (Gouk, 223–245)
- Harmonics as model science 16th–early 18th c. Fludd Utriusque cosmi (1617–21): divine monochord, neo-Platonic sympathy, tonos; Mersenne vs Fludd. Kepler Harmonices mundi (1619): planetary scales from angular velocities at aphelion/perihelion; just (syntonic diatonic) ratios. Newton: colours/spectrum divided like the musical octave (Opticks), Hooke objections; c.1694 Principia scholia on Pythagorean harmony & inverse square law. Human harmonies: Cheyne (musical body/nerves), iatromechanics. Music loses status as intellectual model; Diderot Encyclopédie (1751) Bacon's tree.
## Ch9 Acoustics to Tonpsychologie (Green & Butler, 246–271)
- Sympathetic resonance (antiquity onward). Beeckman (1588–1637) & Galileo (1564–1642): pitch = frequency; Mersenne (1588–1648) Harmonie universelle: laws of strings (length, tension, mass), heard upper partials. Sauveur (1653–1716): harmonic series, nodes, fundamental, 1701 "acoustique". Wallis/Noble & Pigot nodes 1677. Rameau corps sonore (1722 senario → 1726/1737 harmonic series; minor triad problem). Beats (Mersenne), combination/difference tones (Sorge 1745, Tartini 1754 terzo suono, Romieu). Daniel Bernoulli superposition; Ohm's acoustic law (1843); Fourier analysis. Helmholtz (1821–94) Lehre von den Tonempfindungen (1863; Ellis trans. 1875 "On the Sensations of Tone"): resonance theory of hearing (basilar membrane), dissonance = roughness from beats of partials, consonance as absence. Stumpf (1848–1936) Tonpsychologie (1883–90): tonal fusion (Verschmelzung) degrees (octave > fifth > fourth > thirds…). Wundt, Brentano, Husserl, Gestalt (Wertheimer, Köhler), Ebbinghaus.
## Ch10 Theory and mathematics (Nolan, 272–306)
- Pythagoras at the forge (Nicomachus Manual ch. 6; physically false); tetractys; numbers as reality. Senario: Zarlino (1–6) vs Galilei Discorso (1589) physical explanation. Geometric division of time (mensural notation). Geometric imagery: Boethius, Johannes de Muris Musica speculativa (1325); Descartes; Euler's Tonnetz Speculum musicum (1739/1773); Riemann Tonnetz. Combinatorics: Mersenne & Kircher arca musarithmica; Euler gradus suavitatis (1739). 19th-c. Vincent, Loquin, Bacon (pitch-class anticipations). Set & group theory: Babbitt (1946 dissertation; 1960 "Twelve-tone invariants"; combinatoriality), Forte Structure of Atonal Music (1973), Lewin Generalized Musical Intervals and Transformations (1987; GIS, transformation networks); neo-Riemannian. Prescriptive applications (Schillinger, Xenakis).
## Ch11 Notes, scales, modes earlier MA (D. Cohen, 307–363)
- Discrete pitch + scale as the basis. Carolingian renovatio: integrating cantus (chant) and harmonics traditions. Eight modes (toni) from Byzantine oktoechos; Greek-ish names (protus, deuterus, tritus, tetrardus; authentic/plagal) in earliest tonaries (Saint-Riquier c. 800) and Musica disciplina (Aurelian). Modus/tropus terminology. Three systematic sources late 9th/10th c.: Hucbald De harmonica institutione (c. 880–900: nine intervals, letters, tetrachord of the finals D E F G, beginnings & endings), Enchiriadis (daseian), Alia musica (Greek octave-species names applied to the modes → "Dorian" = mode 1 etc.; the misapplication of Ptolemy's/Boethius's names). Later: Dialogus de musica (c. 1000; letter names A–G, gamma, bb/bn, modes by final and ambitus), Guido of Arezzo (Micrologus c. 1026–28; Prologus in antiphonarium (staff lines, F & C), Epistola ad Michahelem (ut-re-mi syllables from Ut queant laxis)), hexachord & Guidonian hand (later), affinities (maneriae) and confinales, species of 4th & 5th defining modes (Hermannus Contractus), ambitus.
## Ch12 Renaissance modal theory (Judd, 364–406)
- "Mode" richly problematic; relation to polyphony; Zarlino as lens. Cantorinus manuals (practical); Vanneus Recanetum (1533) after Gaffurio Practica (1496): psalm-tone mnemonics; mode ethos (Vanneus: mode 2 tearful, 3 harsh, 4 adulatory, 5 jocund, 6 pious...). Tinctoris Liber de natura et proprietate tonorum (1476): mode in polyphony judged by tenor. Pietro Aron Trattato della natura et cognizione di tutti gli tuoni (1525): mode of polyphony by tenor final, ambitus, species, cadences. Glarean Dodecachordon (1547): twelve modes adding Aeolian (A) & Ionian (C) and plagals, examples from printed polyphony (Josquin). Zarlino Le istitutioni harmoniche (1558; Part IV on modes; renumbered modes starting on C in 1571/1573 Dimostrationi). Printed collections ordered by mode (e.g., Palestrina's Offertories?). Modal cadences (regular/irregular). Editorial perspectives: musica ficta etc.
