# Notes ch. 0–4 (book pages)
## Intro (Christensen, 1–26)
- Dahlhaus: theory "resists its own history"; subject shifted. Three texts c.1618: Campion (practical 4-part rule), Descartes Compendium (c.1618, pub. 1650; rational/empirical), Fludd Utriusque cosmi (1617–21; cosmological). Theoria (contemplation) vs praxis (Aristotle); musica theorica (quadrivium) vs musica practica. 19th c. conservatories fracture theory; 20th c. academic theory (neo-Riemannian etc.). Volume organized: I Disciplining; II Speculative traditions; III Regulative (A tonal space, B compositional theory, C time, D tonality); IV Descriptive (A models of analysis, B psychology). Pythagoras at the forge as founding myth. Kessel (c.1766–1823). Goethe "grey is all theory".
## Ch1 Mapping the terrain (Blasius, 27–45)
- Theory maps/schemas. Aristides Quintilianus On Music (early 4th c. CE) first full architecture (theoretical: physical/technical — harmonics, rhythmics, metrics; practical: composition/performance). Marchetto of Padua Lucidarium (1309–18) phenomenal schema. 16th c. synthetic mapping (Gaffurio Theorica musice 1492 parts). Mersenne Harmonie universelle (1636–37; physics/mechanics, 19 books) vs Kircher Musurgia universalis (1650; similitudes, magic, combinatorics). Enlightenment: Forkel's grammar vs rhetoric map. Adler (1885) historical vs systematic musicology, compared himself with Aristides.
## Ch2 Musica practica (Wason, 46–77)
- Integration of practica with theorica. Medieval pedagogy: Carolingian practicality, Guido; university (Johannes de Muris Musica speculativa at Paris). Renaissance compositional pedagogy (Tinctoris, Zarlino's Istitutioni 1558 parts 3–4), German Lateinschule texts. Thoroughbass: Heinichen (1728) complete composition through thoroughbass. Classical era: Riepel, Koch (Versuch 1782–93) melody/phrase grammar & rhetoric. Three national traditions (French fundamental bass, Italian species counterpoint/partimento, German thoroughbass) blend c.1800. Paris Conservatoire (1795): Catel Traité (1802), Reicha. Vienna: Sechter (1788–1867; Schubert lesson; Grundsätze 1853–54). Berlin: A. B. Marx (1795–1866) Kompositionslehre, Formenlehre, Pestalozzi. England/N. America translations. 20th c.: Louis & Thuille Harmonielehre (1906); Schoenberg Harmonielehre (1911); Schenker (posthumous American influence); Boulanger; Hindemith at Yale; Babbitt Princeton PhD and Perspectives of New Music; theory as academic discipline (SMT 1977) — loss of link to living composition.
## Ch3 Epistemologies (Cook, 78–108)
- Dahlhaus's three traditions (speculative/regulative/analytical). Theory once within theological epistemology (Pythagorean world view; Schillinger as anachronism). "Between art and nature": Rameau's claims of natural basis (corps sonore); Sauveur hearing 128th partial; Momigny; Foucault's epistemes. Performative turn: Rameau established discursive space; theory as regulated by intersubjectivity, not objective truth. Conclusion: transition outer→inner (natural science → psychology → phenomenology); GTTM (Lerdahl & Jackendoff 1983) formal grammar of experienced listener; Krumhansl's probe-tone experiments; plural epistemologies.
## Ch4 Greek music theory (Mathiesen, 109–135)
- Corpus table: Division of the Canon (attr. Euclid, 4th–3rd c. BCE); Cleonides Introduction to Harmonics (2nd c. CE); Nicomachus Manual (fl. 100–150 CE); Theon of Smyrna; Ptolemy Harmonics (fl. 127–148); Gaudentius; Porphyry commentary on Ptolemy; Aristides Quintilianus On Music (late 3rd–mid 4th c.); Bacchius; Alypius (notation tables). Aristoxenus Harmonic Elements (4th c. BCE). Meibom edition 1652; humanists Gaffurio, Mei, Galilei, Zarlino.
- Three traditions: Pythagorean (number, cosmos, ethos; tetractys 1:2:3:4; octave 2:1, fifth 3:2, fourth 4:3, tone 9:8; limma 256:243, apotome 2187:2048; arithmetic & harmonic means (Archytas; Plato Timaeus world-soul)); Harmonicists (single genus in an octave, smallest-interval grids, criticized by Aristoxenus); Aristoxenian (ear-based, continuous pitch space, tone = 12 parts; tetrachord genera enharmonic, chromatic, diatonic with shades (Cleonides: enharmonic 3+3+24, soft chromatic 4+4+22, hemiolic 4½+4½+21, tonic 6+6+18, soft diatonic 6+9+15, intense diatonic 6+12+12, in 30-part fourth).
- Archytas's three genera (ratios). Greater Perfect System (two octaves, notes proslambanomenos…nete hyperbolaion, mese central), Lesser Perfect System (synemmenon). Tonoi: Aristoxenus 13 (younger theorists 15), Ptolemy 7 tonoi (octave species). Harmoniai of Plato/Aristotle (ethos). Aristides' six early harmoniai (gapped). Conclusion: transmission via Martianus Capella, Boethius, Cassiodorus.
